About

I am a PhD candidate in Civil Engineering at the University of Arizona, working in the Smart Transportation Laboratory (STL) under the direction of Dr. Yao-Jan Wu. My dissertation focuses on Emerging Technologies for Resilient Highway Operations: Implications for Traffic Predictions and Socially Compatible Driving.

My research integrates transportation operations, safety, and intelligent transportation systems (ITS) to better understand and respond to real-world disruptions. I combine empirical data with advanced modeling and machine learning techniques to evaluate how environmental conditions, emerging vehicle technologies, and operational strategies impact roadway performance.

I am a recipient of the NSF Graduate Research Fellowship and the Dwight David Eisenhower Transportation Fellowship (FHWA), and a 2026 winner of the NOCoE TRB Student Contest.

Current Work

My active research examines how driver-assistance-enabled electric vehicles affect safety, mobility, and environmental outcomes on Arizona highways, alongside developing spatio-temporal forecasting models for post-incident traffic speed recovery. I also serve as Lab Manager of the Smart Transportation Laboratory and contribute to ADOT- and NCHRP-sponsored projects on AI applications for transportation systems management.
